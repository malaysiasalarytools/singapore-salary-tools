Singapore Tax Relief Guide

Learn how tax reliefs reduce your chargeable income, what common reliefs employees may claim, and what to enter in the estimated annual tax relief field of our Singapore salary calculator.

How tax relief affects your tax

Singapore income tax is usually calculated on chargeable income. In simple terms: annual income minus allowable deductions and personal reliefs equals chargeable income. Lower chargeable income usually means lower tax payable.

What are tax reliefs in Singapore?

Tax reliefs are deductions allowed to eligible individuals. They do not directly reduce your tax dollar-for-dollar. Instead, they reduce the amount of income that is taxed.

Reliefs reduce income

A S$1,000 relief reduces your chargeable income by S$1,000. The actual tax saving depends on your tax bracket.

Eligibility matters

Some reliefs apply automatically, while others depend on your family situation, CPF contribution, SRS contribution or expenses.

There is a relief cap

Total personal income tax reliefs are generally capped at S$80,000 per Year of Assessment.

Common Singapore tax reliefs

These are common reliefs that employees may see when planning Singapore personal income tax. Always check your actual IRAS tax assessment for the final approved amount.

Tax Relief Common Amount / Basis Who It May Apply To
Earned Income Relief Commonly S$1,000 for individuals below 55, with higher amounts for older taxpayers. Tax residents who have earned income from employment, trade, business, profession or vocation.
CPF Relief Based on compulsory employee CPF contributions and certain approved voluntary CPF contributions. Singapore Citizens and PRs who make qualifying CPF contributions.
Spouse Relief Commonly S$2,000 if qualifying conditions are met. Taxpayers supporting a spouse with income not exceeding the relevant threshold.
Parent Relief Amount depends on whether the dependant lives with you and whether the dependant is handicapped. Taxpayers supporting parents, grandparents, parents-in-law or grandparents-in-law who meet the conditions.
Qualifying Child Relief Commonly S$4,000 per qualifying child. Parents supporting a child who meets the qualifying conditions.
Working Mother’s Child Relief Based on a percentage of the mother’s earned income, subject to limits. Working mothers who are Singapore tax residents and meet the qualifying conditions.
Course Fees Relief Relief may be available for qualifying course fees, subject to a cap. Individuals who paid for approved courses related to their employment, trade or professional development.
SRS Relief Based on qualifying Supplementary Retirement Scheme contributions, subject to annual contribution caps. Individuals who contribute to an SRS account for retirement planning.

How to estimate your annual tax relief

If you are using the salary calculator and are not sure what number to enter, start with reliefs you are confident about.

Step 1: Start with CPF Relief For most Singapore Citizen or PR employees, compulsory employee CPF contributions are usually an important tax relief. If your salary calculator already deducts employee CPF, you can use your annual employee CPF as a rough starting point.
Step 2: Add Earned Income Relief If you are a Singapore tax resident with employment income, you may generally include Earned Income Relief based on your age group.
Step 3: Add family-related reliefs only if eligible Include spouse, parent, child or working mother reliefs only if you meet the conditions. Do not add them just because you support family expenses casually.
Step 4: Keep the estimate conservative If unsure, enter a lower amount or S$0. This avoids underestimating your annual tax payable.

Simple example

This example shows how reliefs affect chargeable income. The exact tax payable still depends on Singapore resident tax rates.

Item Example Amount
Annual employment income S$60,000
Less: estimated employee CPF Relief S$12,000
Less: Earned Income Relief S$1,000
Estimated chargeable income S$47,000

In this example, the taxpayer does not pay tax on the full S$60,000. The tax is estimated on the chargeable income after reliefs.

Tax reliefs you should not blindly include

Some reliefs are commonly misunderstood. Only include them if you meet the qualifying conditions.

Parent Relief

You usually need to support the dependant and meet income, relationship and residency conditions.

Spouse Relief

This does not apply to every married taxpayer. Your spouse must meet the relevant income and support conditions.

Course Fees Relief

Not every course qualifies. Personal interest courses may not be claimable.

Resident vs non-resident tax relief

Singapore tax reliefs are generally relevant for tax residents. Non-resident employment income is commonly taxed differently, and personal reliefs are usually not applied in the same way.

If you choose “Non-resident employment income” in our salary calculator, the estimated annual tax relief field is disabled and set to S$0. This keeps the estimate cleaner for non-resident tax treatment.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is tax relief in Singapore?

Tax relief is an amount that reduces your chargeable income. It does not directly reduce your tax payable dollar-for-dollar.

Should I enter S$0 if I am not sure about my tax reliefs?

Yes, S$0 is a conservative estimate. Your estimated tax may be higher, but it reduces the risk of underestimating tax payable.

Is CPF Relief the same as employee CPF contribution?

CPF Relief is generally related to qualifying CPF contributions. For employees, compulsory employee CPF contribution is commonly a key part of relief planning.

Do foreigners get Singapore tax reliefs?

It depends on tax residency and the type of relief. Non-resident employment income is usually treated differently, and personal reliefs may not apply in the same way.

What is the S$80,000 personal relief cap?

Singapore generally caps total personal income tax reliefs at S$80,000 per Year of Assessment. If your total reliefs exceed the cap, the excess may not reduce your chargeable income.

Can tax relief reduce my income tax to zero?

Yes, if your chargeable income after reliefs is low enough, your estimated income tax may be zero.

Where can I confirm my actual tax relief amount?

You should check your IRAS tax filing details, Notice of Assessment and official IRAS guidance for your final approved tax relief amount.
