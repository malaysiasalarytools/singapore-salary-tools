Singapore hourly rate guide for employees

Learn how to convert monthly salary to hourly rate, compare full-time and part-time pay, estimate daily rate, and understand how hourly rate is used for overtime and salary planning.

Monthly to Hourly Daily Rate Overtime Rate Salary Comparison

What is hourly rate?

Hourly rate is the amount of pay earned for one hour of work. It is useful for comparing monthly salary, part-time jobs, freelance work, overtime pay and different work schedules.

In Singapore, many full-time employees are paid a fixed monthly salary. However, converting monthly salary into an hourly rate helps you understand the real value of your time and whether a job offer is attractive compared with another role.

Simple idea: Hourly rate helps you compare jobs more fairly because two people with the same monthly salary may work different hours.

How to calculate hourly rate from monthly salary

A common practical estimate is to divide monthly salary by the average number of working hours in a month. For Singapore salary and overtime planning, a common reference is based on 44 hours per week.

Common hourly rate formula: Hourly rate = 12 × monthly basic salary ÷ 52 ÷ weekly working hours

If you use 44 hours per week, this means:

Example formula: Hourly rate = 12 × monthly basic salary ÷ 52 ÷ 44

For example, if your monthly basic salary is S$4,000, the estimated hourly rate is: 12 × S$4,000 ÷ 52 ÷ 44 = about S$20.98 per hour.

Basic salary vs gross salary for hourly rate

Basic salary is your fixed salary before allowances, bonus, overtime and deductions. Gross salary may include basic salary plus allowances, commissions, bonus or other payments.

For overtime-related calculation, hourly basic rate is usually based on basic salary instead of total gross salary. For personal job comparison, you may also calculate an effective hourly rate using total annual compensation.

Practical tip: Use basic salary for overtime-style calculation. Use annual total compensation if you want to compare the full value of two job offers.

How to estimate daily rate

Daily rate is useful for unpaid leave, salary proration, short employment periods and job comparison. A simple estimate is to divide monthly salary by the average number of working days or calendar days, depending on the purpose.

Method Formula Best For
Calendar day method Monthly salary ÷ number of days in the month Simple salary proration by calendar days
Working day method Monthly salary ÷ working days in the month Internal HR or payroll policy comparison
Annual method Monthly salary × 12 ÷ 260 Rough annual working day estimate

Different companies may use different proration policies, so employees should check their employment contract or HR policy when calculating exact final salary or no-pay leave deduction.

Hourly rate and overtime pay

Hourly rate is important for overtime calculation. For employees who are entitled to overtime pay, overtime is generally calculated using hourly basic rate multiplied by the overtime rate.

Overtime pay formula: Overtime pay = hourly basic rate × 1.5 × overtime hours

For example, if your hourly basic rate is S$15 and you work 10 overtime hours, estimated overtime pay is S$15 × 1.5 × 10 = S$225.

Hourly rate for part-time employees

For part-time employees, hourly rate is usually stated directly in the employment arrangement. If you work irregular hours, hourly rate makes it easier to calculate monthly pay based on actual hours worked.

Example: If your hourly rate is S$12 and you work 80 hours in a month, your gross pay is S$960 before CPF, tax or other deductions, if applicable.

Hourly rate for freelance or contract work

Freelancers and contract workers should not compare hourly rate with employee salary directly without considering CPF, leave, medical benefits, insurance, unpaid time, admin work and business costs.

A freelancer may need to charge a higher hourly rate than an employee because they may not receive paid annual leave, CPF employer contribution, paid sick leave, bonus or other employment benefits.

Common hourly rate mistakes

Ignoring working hours

A higher monthly salary may be less attractive if the working hours are much longer.

Using gross salary for OT

Overtime is usually based on hourly basic rate, not full gross salary with all allowances.

Forgetting benefits

Freelance hourly rate should be compared after considering leave, CPF and benefits.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate hourly rate from monthly salary in Singapore?

A common estimate is 12 × monthly basic salary ÷ 52 ÷ weekly working hours. If you use 44 hours per week, this gives a practical hourly basic rate estimate.

What is the hourly rate for S$4,000 monthly salary?

Using 44 hours per week, S$4,000 monthly salary is about S$20.98 per hour.

Should I use basic salary or gross salary?

For overtime-style calculation, basic salary is usually more appropriate. For job comparison, you may use total annual compensation to estimate an effective hourly rate.

How do I calculate daily rate from monthly salary?

Daily rate can be estimated using calendar days, working days or annual working days. The right method depends on whether you are calculating salary proration, no-pay leave or general comparison.

Is hourly rate used for overtime pay?

Yes. Overtime pay is generally based on hourly basic rate multiplied by the overtime rate and number of overtime hours.

Is a higher monthly salary always better?

Not always. You should also compare working hours, CPF, leave, bonus, benefits and workload. Hourly rate helps you compare the real value of each job offer.

How do part-time employees calculate monthly salary?

For part-time employees, monthly pay is usually hourly rate multiplied by actual hours worked, before CPF, tax or other deductions where applicable.

Should freelancers charge the same hourly rate as employees?

Usually no. Freelancers often need a higher hourly rate because they may not receive employer CPF, paid leave, sick leave, bonus or other employee benefits.

Can I use hourly rate to compare job offers?

Yes. Hourly rate is useful for comparing job offers with different working hours, but you should also consider benefits, bonus, CPF, career growth and work-life balance.

Is this hourly rate guide official advice?

No. This guide is for general salary planning only. Check MOM, CPF Board, IRAS or your employment contract for official treatment.
