How this Singapore salary calculator works

Your salary in Singapore is usually not the same as your final monthly cash received. The main difference comes from employee CPF contribution and income tax. This calculator separates gross salary, employee CPF, employer CPF and estimated tax so you can understand your real take-home pay more clearly.

1. Start with gross salary

Gross salary is your salary before CPF, income tax and other payroll deductions. For most employees, this is the monthly amount stated in the employment contract or offer letter before deductions.

2. Estimate CPF

Employee CPF is deducted from your salary, while employer CPF is paid separately by your employer. Employer CPF increases your total employment package but does not form part of your cash take-home pay.

3. Estimate income tax

Singapore income tax is calculated annually. This calculator estimates your annual tax and spreads it over 12 months so you can compare salary after CPF and tax more easily.

Salary after CPF vs salary after tax

Salary after CPF and salary after tax are not exactly the same. Salary after CPF shows your monthly cash before considering income tax. Salary after tax gives a more conservative view by spreading estimated annual income tax over each month. This is useful when planning rent, housing loan affordability, savings, insurance, family expenses or job offer comparisons.

Salary after CPF

This is your gross monthly salary minus employee CPF contribution. For example, if your salary is S$5,000 and your employee CPF rate is 20%, your salary after CPF is about S$4,000 before tax.

Salary after tax

This deducts estimated income tax as well. Since Singapore tax is assessed yearly, your monthly salary may not have tax deducted immediately, but you should still plan for the annual tax payable.

Total compensation

Employer CPF is not deducted from your salary. It is an additional employer contribution on top of your gross salary, so it helps you understand the full cost and value of your employment package.

Popular Singapore salary scenarios

Many employees want to know how much cash they can actually receive from common monthly salary levels. The examples below assume an employee aged 55 and below, Singapore Citizen / SPR third year onwards, with standard employee CPF contribution and no additional tax reliefs.

Salary after CPF on S$3,000

On a S$3,000 monthly salary, employee CPF is estimated at about S$600. This gives an approximate monthly take-home before tax of S$2,400. This salary level is commonly searched by fresh graduates, junior executives and entry-level employees.

Salary after CPF on S$4,000

On a S$4,000 monthly salary, employee CPF is estimated at about S$800. The approximate monthly take-home before tax is S$3,200. This is a common benchmark for early-career professionals comparing job offers in Singapore.

Salary after CPF on S$5,000

On a S$5,000 monthly salary, employee CPF is estimated at about S$1,000. The approximate monthly take-home before tax is S$4,000. Many employees use this benchmark to check affordability for rent, savings and monthly expenses.

Salary after CPF on S$6,000

On a S$6,000 monthly salary, employee CPF is estimated at about S$1,200. The approximate monthly take-home before tax is S$4,800. At this level, annual income tax may become more meaningful depending on reliefs, bonuses and other income.

Salary after CPF on S$8,000

On a S$8,000 monthly salary, employee CPF is estimated at about S$1,600. The approximate monthly take-home before tax is S$6,400. This is also important because S$8,000 is the Ordinary Wage ceiling used for monthly CPF calculation in 2026.

Salary after CPF on S$10,000

On a S$10,000 monthly salary, employee CPF is still estimated based on the CPF Ordinary Wage ceiling. This means employee CPF on Ordinary Wages may not continue increasing after the monthly ceiling, although tax payable will usually be higher due to higher annual income.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate salary after CPF in Singapore?

Salary after CPF is calculated by taking your gross monthly salary and deducting your employee CPF contribution. For example, if your gross salary is S$5,000 and your employee CPF contribution is S$1,000, your salary after CPF is about S$4,000 before income tax.

How do I calculate take-home pay in Singapore?

Take-home pay is your gross salary minus employee CPF contributions and estimated income tax. This calculator estimates both CPF and income tax to show your approximate net salary.

Does this calculator include CPF contributions?

Yes. The calculator estimates both employee CPF deductions and employer CPF contributions based on your age group and salary.

How much CPF do employees contribute in Singapore?

CPF contribution rates vary by age. Employees aged 55 and below generally contribute up to 20% of eligible wages, while employers contribute up to 17% separately.

How much CPF does my employer contribute?

Employer CPF contributions are paid separately by your employer and do not reduce your salary. The amount depends on your age, wage level and CPF eligibility.

Is CPF calculated on the full salary?

CPF contributions are subject to CPF wage ceilings. If your salary exceeds the CPF Ordinary Wage ceiling, CPF will only be calculated up to the applicable limit for Ordinary Wages.

Is income tax deducted monthly in Singapore?

Singapore income tax is usually assessed annually by IRAS. This calculator converts estimated annual tax into a monthly amount for easier salary planning.

Does this calculator include bonuses?

Yes. You can enter your annual bonus to estimate your annual income and income tax impact. CPF on bonuses may be subject to Additional Wage rules, so the result is still an estimate.

Can foreigners use this salary calculator?

Yes. However, many foreigners working in Singapore do not contribute CPF. Their take-home pay may therefore differ from Singapore Citizens and Permanent Residents.

Is S$4,000 a good salary in Singapore?

S$4,000 per month is often considered a decent early-career salary. After employee CPF contribution, the monthly take-home before tax may be around S$3,200 for an employee aged 55 and below.

Is S$5,000 a good salary in Singapore?

S$5,000 per month can be comfortable for a single person depending on rent, lifestyle and savings goals. After employee CPF contribution, the monthly take-home before tax may be around S$4,000.

How much CPF do I pay on a S$5,000 salary?

For employees aged 55 and below, CPF employee contributions are generally 20% of eligible wages. On a monthly salary of S$5,000, the employee CPF contribution is approximately S$1,000, while the employer contributes approximately S$850 separately.

How much tax do I pay on a S$60,000 salary in Singapore?

The amount of income tax payable depends on your tax residency status, CPF contributions, reliefs, rebates and deductions. Use the calculator above for a personalised estimate.

Does overtime pay affect CPF and income tax?

In many cases, overtime pay is considered part of employment income and may affect CPF contributions and taxable income. The exact treatment depends on your employment arrangement and payroll structure.

Does salary increment increase CPF contributions?

Yes. A higher salary generally results in higher CPF contributions, subject to CPF wage ceilings. It may also increase annual taxable income.

How accurate is this Singapore Salary Calculator?

This calculator provides an estimate based on simplified CPF and income tax rules. Actual results may vary depending on CPF eligibility, tax reliefs, deductions, rebates, bonuses and employment arrangements.
