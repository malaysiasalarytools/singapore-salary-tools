How this Singapore salary calculator works

1. Start with gross salary

Your gross monthly salary is the amount before CPF, income tax and other deductions.

2. Estimate CPF

Employee CPF is deducted from your salary. Employer CPF is paid by your employer on top of your salary, subject to CPF rules and wage ceilings.

3. Estimate tax

Singapore income tax is calculated annually. This calculator spreads the estimated annual tax over 12 months to show a monthly view.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate my take-home pay in Singapore?

Take-home pay is your gross salary minus employee CPF contributions and any income tax payable. This calculator estimates both CPF and income tax to show your net salary.

Does this calculator include CPF contributions?

Yes. The calculator estimates both employee CPF deductions and employer CPF contributions based on your age group and salary.

Is CPF calculated on the full salary?

CPF contributions are subject to CPF wage ceilings. If your salary exceeds the CPF Ordinary Wage ceiling, CPF will only be calculated up to the applicable limit.

How much CPF do employees contribute in Singapore?

CPF contribution rates vary by age. Employees aged 55 and below generally contribute up to 20% of eligible wages, while employers contribute up to 17%.

How much CPF does my employer contribute?

Employer CPF contributions are paid separately by your employer and do not reduce your salary. The amount depends on your age and CPF eligibility.

Is income tax deducted monthly in Singapore?

Singapore income tax is usually assessed annually by IRAS. This calculator converts the estimated annual tax into a monthly amount for easier salary planning.

Does this calculator include bonuses?

Yes. You can enter your annual bonus to estimate your annual income, CPF impact and tax payable.

Can foreigners use this salary calculator?

Yes. However, most foreigners working in Singapore do not contribute CPF. Their take-home pay may therefore differ significantly from Singapore Citizens and Permanent Residents.

How accurate is this Singapore Salary Calculator?

This calculator provides an estimate based on publicly available CPF and income tax rules. Actual results may vary depending on tax reliefs, deductions, bonuses and employment arrangements.

What salary do I need to take home S$5,000 per month?

The answer depends on your age, CPF contribution rates and tax situation. Enter different salary amounts to estimate the gross salary required.

Does overtime pay affect CPF and income tax?

In many cases, overtime pay is considered part of your employment income and may affect CPF contributions and taxable income.

Does salary increment increase CPF contributions?

Yes. A higher salary generally results in higher CPF contributions, subject to CPF wage ceilings.

What is a good salary in Singapore?

A good salary depends on your lifestyle, family size and housing situation. For a single person, many consider a monthly salary between S$4,000 and S$6,000 sufficient for a comfortable lifestyle, while higher incomes may be needed for home ownership and family expenses.

Is S$4,000 a good salary in Singapore?

S$4,000 per month is generally considered a decent entry-level to mid-level salary in Singapore. After CPF contributions, your take-home pay may be around S$3,200, depending on your age and tax situation.

Is S$5,000 a good salary in Singapore?

S$5,000 per month is above Singapore's median employee income and is often considered a comfortable salary for a single person. Your actual take-home pay will depend on CPF contributions, taxes and personal expenses.

How much CPF do I pay on a S$5,000 salary?

For employees aged 55 and below, CPF employee contributions are generally 20% of eligible wages. On a monthly salary of S$5,000, the employee CPF contribution is approximately S$1,000, while the employer contributes approximately S$850 separately.

How much tax do I pay on a S$60,000 salary in Singapore?

The amount of income tax payable depends on your tax residency status, CPF contributions and tax reliefs. For many tax residents earning around S$60,000 annually, income tax is relatively low compared with many countries. Use the calculator above for a personalised estimate.

What salary is considered middle class in Singapore?

Middle-class income levels vary by household size and lifestyle. Many Singaporeans consider monthly incomes between S$4,000 and S$8,000 per person to fall within the middle-income range.

What salary is needed to live comfortably in Singapore?

The amount needed depends on housing, transport and lifestyle choices. Single individuals often aim for at least S$4,000 to S$6,000 monthly, while families may require significantly more.

How much salary increase should I expect in Singapore?

Annual salary increments vary by industry, performance and economic conditions. Many employees receive salary increases ranging from a few percent to double-digit percentages depending on circumstances.
