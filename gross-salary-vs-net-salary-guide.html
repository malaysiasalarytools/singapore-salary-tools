Gross salary vs net salary in Singapore

Learn the difference between gross salary, basic salary, net salary and take-home pay. Understand how CPF, tax, allowances, bonus and deductions affect the final amount you receive.

Gross Salary Net Salary CPF Deductions Take-Home Pay

What is the difference between gross salary and net salary?

Gross salary is your salary before deductions. Net salary is the final amount you receive after deductions such as employee CPF contribution, unpaid leave deduction, salary advance recovery or other authorised deductions.

In Singapore, employees often compare salary using gross monthly salary, but the amount that matters for monthly budgeting is usually net salary or take-home pay.

Simple rule: Gross salary is before deductions. Net salary is after deductions. Take-home pay usually means the cash amount you receive in your bank account.

Gross salary, basic salary and net salary explained

Gross salary to net salary formula

A simple way to estimate net salary is to start with gross salary and deduct employee CPF and other salary deductions.

Simple net salary formula: Net salary = Gross salary − employee CPF − other deductions

For Singapore Citizens and Permanent Residents who contribute CPF, employee CPF is usually one of the biggest deductions from gross salary. For foreigners who do not contribute CPF, the difference between gross and net salary may be smaller, unless there are other deductions.

How CPF affects net salary

CPF is a major reason why gross salary and net salary are different in Singapore. Employee CPF is deducted from your salary before your net pay is credited to your bank account.

Employer CPF is different. It is paid separately by your employer and does not reduce your monthly cash salary. This means your payslip may show both employee CPF and employer CPF, but only employee CPF is deducted from your gross salary.

Example: If your gross salary is S$5,000 and employee CPF is S$1,000, your estimated net pay before tax and other deductions is about S$4,000.

Does income tax reduce monthly net salary?

Singapore personal income tax is generally assessed annually. For many employees, income tax is not deducted from monthly salary in the same way CPF is deducted. Instead, tax is assessed by IRAS based on annual income, tax residency and reliefs.

However, income tax still matters for personal budgeting. Even if your monthly net salary looks higher, you may need to set aside money for your annual tax bill.

Planning tip: Take-home salary for monthly cash flow and annual income tax are connected, but they are not always deducted at the same time.

Are allowances part of gross salary?

Some allowances may be included in gross salary, especially fixed monthly allowances such as transport allowance, meal allowance or shift allowance. However, not every allowance is treated the same for CPF, tax or payroll purposes.

When comparing salary packages, check whether the offer states basic salary only or gross salary including allowances. A higher gross salary may not always mean a higher basic salary.

Does bonus affect gross salary or net salary?

Bonus is additional employment income paid on top of normal salary. It may affect annual gross income, CPF contributions and income tax. Bonus may also change your effective annual net income even if your monthly net salary stays the same.

For Singapore Citizens and PRs, CPF may apply to bonus subject to Additional Wage ceiling rules. Bonus is also generally taxable as employment income.

Gross vs net salary for foreigners in Singapore

Many foreign employees in Singapore do not contribute CPF. This means their monthly net salary may be closer to gross salary, unless there are other deductions such as unpaid leave, salary advance recovery or employee benefits deductions.

However, foreigners still need to consider income tax, tax residency, employment pass costs, rent, insurance, medical costs and other living expenses when planning take-home income.

Common salary comparison mistakes

Comparing gross only

Gross salary does not show how much cash you actually receive after deductions.

Ignoring CPF

Employee CPF can significantly reduce monthly cash salary for eligible employees.

Forgetting annual tax

Income tax may not be deducted monthly, but it still affects annual take-home income.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is gross salary in Singapore?

Gross salary is salary before deductions. It may include basic salary and fixed allowances, depending on how the employer defines the salary package.

What is net salary in Singapore?

Net salary is the amount after deductions such as employee CPF and other authorised deductions. It is close to what employees usually call take-home pay.

Is basic salary the same as gross salary?

Not always. Basic salary is the fixed base salary. Gross salary may include basic salary plus allowances or other regular payments.

Why is my net salary lower than my gross salary?

Net salary is lower because deductions such as employee CPF, unpaid leave, absence deductions or other approved deductions may be deducted from gross salary.

Does CPF reduce net salary?

Yes. Employee CPF is deducted from gross salary and reduces monthly cash take-home pay. Employer CPF is paid separately and does not reduce your salary.

Is income tax deducted from monthly salary in Singapore?

Singapore income tax is generally assessed annually. It may not be deducted from monthly salary like CPF, but it still affects your annual take-home income.

Do foreigners have higher net salary because there is no CPF?

Many foreigners do not contribute CPF, so monthly net salary may be closer to gross salary. However, tax, living costs, insurance and other deductions should still be considered.

Should I compare job offers using gross or net salary?

Compare both. Gross salary helps compare offer size, while net salary and annual take-home pay show your practical cash flow.

Does bonus count as gross salary?

Bonus is usually additional employment income. It affects annual gross income and may also affect CPF and tax, depending on the situation.

Is this gross vs net salary guide official advice?

No. This guide is for general salary planning only. Check CPF Board, IRAS, MOM or your employment contract for official treatment.
