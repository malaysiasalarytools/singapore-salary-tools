Quick answer

For Singapore Citizens and Singapore Permanent Residents from the third year onwards, employees aged 55 and below generally contribute 20% of eligible monthly wages to CPF, while employers contribute 17%. CPF is subject to wage ceilings, so CPF is not always calculated on the full salary if the salary is above the applicable ceiling.

What is CPF?

CPF stands for Central Provident Fund. It is Singapore’s social security savings system for eligible employees. CPF contributions are usually made by both the employee and the employer. The employee’s CPF contribution is deducted from gross salary, while the employer’s CPF contribution is paid on top of salary.

Employee CPF

This is deducted from your gross salary and credited into your CPF accounts. It reduces your monthly cash take-home pay.

Employer CPF

This is contributed separately by your employer. It is part of your employment cost, but it is not deducted from your salary.

CPF wage ceiling

CPF contributions are subject to wage ceilings. For 2026, the Ordinary Wage ceiling is S$8,000 per month.

CPF contribution rates from 1 January 2026

The table below summarises the CPF contribution rates for Singapore Citizens and Singapore Permanent Residents from the third year onwards, where monthly wages are more than S$750.

CPF wage ceiling in 2026

CPF is not always calculated on your full monthly salary. Ordinary Wages are subject to the Ordinary Wage ceiling. For 2026, the Ordinary Wage ceiling is S$8,000 per month. This means that if your monthly salary is higher than S$8,000, CPF on Ordinary Wages is generally calculated up to S$8,000 only.

Salary below S$8,000

If your monthly salary is below the Ordinary Wage ceiling, CPF is generally calculated based on your actual eligible monthly wages.

Salary at S$8,000

If your monthly salary is S$8,000, CPF is generally calculated on S$8,000 of Ordinary Wages.

Salary above S$8,000

If your monthly salary is above S$8,000, CPF on Ordinary Wages is generally capped at the S$8,000 ceiling.

Who needs to contribute CPF?

CPF generally applies to Singapore Citizens and Singapore Permanent Residents who are working in Singapore under an employer-employee relationship. Foreigners working in Singapore usually do not contribute CPF.

Singapore Citizens

Singapore Citizens who are employees generally contribute CPF if they meet the CPF contribution conditions.

Singapore PRs

PRs generally contribute CPF, but first-year and second-year PRs may be subject to graduated contribution rates.

Foreign employees

Foreigners working in Singapore generally do not contribute CPF. Their salary planning may mainly involve income tax and other deductions.

Employee CPF vs employer CPF

A common misunderstanding is that employer CPF is deducted from salary. In normal salary presentation, employee CPF reduces your cash salary, while employer CPF is an additional contribution paid by the employer.

Gross salary

This is your salary before employee CPF, tax and other deductions. Example: S$5,000 gross monthly salary.

Employee CPF deduction

If employee CPF is S$1,000, your cash salary before tax may become around S$4,000.

Employer CPF contribution

Employer CPF is paid separately by your employer. For S$5,000 salary at 17%, this may be around S$850.

CPF and take-home pay

Your monthly take-home pay is affected by employee CPF because employee CPF is deducted from gross salary. Employer CPF does not reduce your monthly salary, but it increases your total employment benefit.

How CPF moves from salary to your CPF accounts

For a CPF-eligible employee, employee CPF is deducted from wages while employer CPF is paid by the employer in addition to cash salary. The combined contribution is credited to CPF accounts according to the allocation rules that apply to the member's age. This is why employer CPF should not be treated as a second deduction from take-home pay.

For salary planning, separate four numbers: gross salary, employee CPF deduction, cash salary after employee CPF, and employer CPF contribution. Use the CPF Calculator to see the breakdown.

Ordinary Wages vs Additional Wages

CPF distinguishes regular Ordinary Wages (OW), such as salary due for the month, from Additional Wages (AW), such as bonuses that are not granted wholly and exclusively for that month. In 2026, the OW ceiling is S$8,000 per month. The annual salary ceiling remains S$102,000.

The AW ceiling is calculated as S$102,000 minus total OW subject to CPF for the year. This matters when estimating CPF on a bonus or AWS. See the Singapore Bonus Guide and AWS / 13th Month Guide for common bonus situations.

CPF for Singapore Permanent Residents

Singapore PRs generally contribute at graduated rates during the first two years of SPR status and at full rates from the third year onwards. The first year begins on the date SPR status is obtained; the second and third-year periods change after the relevant anniversaries under CPF Board rules. Employers and employees may also jointly apply for certain higher contribution-rate options during the first two years.

Because first- and second-year SPR tables vary by SPR year, age and wage band, check the current CPF Board table for payroll-grade figures. Our CPF Calculator currently focuses on Singapore Citizens and SPRs from the third year onwards and labels this scope clearly.

CPF example: S$10,000 monthly salary

For an employee aged 55 or below on full rates, a S$10,000 monthly salary does not mean all S$10,000 is subject to OW CPF. In 2026, OW subject to CPF is capped at S$8,000 for the month. At the 20% employee and 17% employer rates, that gives an illustrative employee contribution of S$1,600 and employer contribution of S$1,360 before considering any special wage-band or other circumstances.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the CPF contribution rates in Singapore for 2026?

For Singapore Citizens and PRs from the third year onwards earning more than S$750 per month, the 2026 CPF rates depend on age. Employees aged 55 and below generally have 17% employer CPF and 20% employee CPF, giving a total CPF rate of 37%.

How much CPF does an employee contribute?

The employee CPF rate depends on age and CPF eligibility. For employees aged 55 and below under full CPF rates, employee CPF is generally 20% of eligible wages, subject to CPF wage ceilings.

How much CPF does an employer contribute?

Employer CPF depends on the employee’s age and CPF eligibility. For employees aged 55 and below under full CPF rates, employer CPF is generally 17% of eligible wages, subject to CPF rules and wage ceilings.

Is CPF calculated on gross salary?

CPF is generally calculated on eligible wages, but it is subject to wage ceilings. If your monthly Ordinary Wage is above the Ordinary Wage ceiling, CPF on Ordinary Wages is calculated only up to the ceiling.

What is the CPF Ordinary Wage ceiling in 2026?

The CPF Ordinary Wage ceiling in 2026 is S$8,000 per month. This affects how much of your monthly Ordinary Wages attracts CPF contributions.

Does employer CPF reduce my take-home salary?

No. Employer CPF is paid separately by your employer. Employee CPF is the part that is deducted from your gross salary and reduces your monthly cash take-home pay.

Do foreigners in Singapore contribute CPF?

Foreign employees in Singapore generally do not contribute CPF. CPF usually applies to Singapore Citizens and Singapore Permanent Residents, subject to CPF rules.

Do Singapore PRs contribute CPF?

Yes, Singapore PRs generally contribute CPF. However, first-year and second-year PRs may have graduated contribution rates, while PRs from the third year onwards generally follow full CPF rates.

How much CPF is deducted from a S$5,000 salary?

For an employee aged 55 and below under full CPF rates, employee CPF on S$5,000 salary is approximately S$1,000. Take-home pay before income tax and other deductions would be around S$4,000.

How much CPF is deducted from a S$10,000 salary?

For Ordinary Wages, CPF is subject to the Ordinary Wage ceiling. If the ceiling is S$8,000, employee CPF at 20% would generally be calculated on S$8,000, resulting in about S$1,600 employee CPF.

No. SG Salary Tools provides simple salary and CPF planning information only. For official CPF rules, contribution rates and special cases, always check CPF Board directly.
